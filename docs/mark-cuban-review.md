# FocusForge v1.0 — The Mark Cuban Review

**A second-pass review in the voice of a brutal, no-bullshit
investor/operator who's seen too many indie apps flame out. Not a real
Mark Cuban review — a simulated one to stress-test the strategy.**

---

## The opening

Look, the work is impressive. Solo dev, polished UI, open-source
engine, hosted privacy policy, custom app icon you wrote in
CoreGraphics. All of that says you can ship.

What it doesn't say is whether you can build a **business**.

I'm going to tell you what I'd tell anyone who pitched me this on
Shark Tank. I don't care if it makes you uncomfortable. The market
doesn't care either.

Let's go.

---

## 1 · How do you make money?

**You don't.**

Your v1.0 is free. No IAP. No subscription. No ads. Zero revenue.

You wrote in your PRD that "v1.1+ optional FocusForge+ subscription"
is on the roadmap. **That's not a plan. That's a hope.**

I've watched a hundred indie devs say "I'll add monetization later
once I have users." Here's what happens: by the time they've
"earned" enough users to feel okay charging, the user base is
trained on "free." The first day they put up a paywall, half their
users leave and the other half write 1-star reviews.

**You don't add monetization. You launch with it.**

If FocusForge is worth $4.99/month — say so on day one. Free trial,
fine. But the paywall has to be there from the App Store description
forward. If your value prop can't justify $5/month, you don't have a
business. You have a hobby.

What I'd want to see:
- Free tier: 3 sessions a day, no character growth, no AI coach
- FocusForge+ at $4.99/mo or $39.99/yr: unlimited sessions, character
  progression, AI coach, streak freezes, cosmetics
- 7-day free trial of FocusForge+ at first launch — capture intent
  early

This isn't even aggressive. Forest charges $3.99 one-time. Be Focused
Pro is $4.99/mo. You're in the price band.

**Action:** Ship FocusForge+ on day 1. Not v1.1. Day 1. Or admit
you're building a portfolio piece, not a business.

## 2 · Market size — be honest

The iOS productivity category has thousands of apps. Forest has
10M+ downloads. Be Focused, Focus Keeper, Bear Focus Timer, Toggl,
Session, Flow — all already in the market. All free or freemium.
All polished.

What's the realistic ceiling? Honestly? Maybe 100K downloads in the
first year if you're lucky and you crush distribution. 5-10% paid
conversion at $50/year ARPU. **$250K-500K ARR best case in year one.**

That's not nothing. It's a lifestyle business. Cool, you make some
money, you keep your independence, you live well. Not bad.

But don't lie to yourself about the size. Don't tell yourself
"if we just hit D7 ≥20% the venture math works." It doesn't.
$500K ARR is not a venture business. You can run that solo and live
well or scale to a small team. Pick one. Don't pretend.

**Action:** decide right now if you're building a lifestyle business
or trying to scale. If lifestyle: optimize for ARPU, not user count.
If scale: get someone else's money in the game because solo $0 cap
won't get you past 100K MAU.

## 3 · Distribution. You don't have it.

Building in public on X with 1,000 followers is **not distribution.
It's networking.**

Real distribution looks like:
- $50K-$200K in App Store Search Ads in year one minimum
- A creator partnership program (people on YouTube/TikTok with
  productivity audiences who'll demo the app for a cut of revenue or
  flat fee)
- Press at launch (TechCrunch, The Verge, Lifehacker — even one hit
  matters)
- ProductHunt #1 day on launch
- Reddit launches in r/productivity, r/getmotivated, r/iosdev
- Bundle deals with similar indie tools

You have **none** of this planned. Your PRD has "X + LinkedIn" as
the marketing channels. That's how a person promotes a portfolio
piece, not how a business gets to 100K users.

The cost of paid acquisition on iOS productivity is **$5-15 per
install** minimum. With $0 ARPU you'll never recover that. So you're
betting on organic. Organic at 1,000 X followers gets you maybe
500 installs at launch. Then what?

**Action:** before submission, you need to lay out a distribution
plan that doesn't depend on your X audience. Three concrete
channels. Numbers. Timeline. If you can't, you're not launching —
you're publishing.

## 4 · The "moat" — let me push back on every one

You think you have moats. Let me list them and tell you why I'd
build around each one in two months:

### Moat 1: "Hand-written coach, not LLM"

So what? Forest could write 33 templates in a week and slap an
"on-device AI" label on them next month. The first time a frontier
model is small enough to ship on-device — and that's already
happening with Apple Intelligence — your hand-written advantage
disappears.

You're racing a glacier that's about to start melting. Your "moat"
is a date that's coming.

### Moat 2: "On-device privacy"

95% of users don't care. The 5% who do are not a market — they're a
political opinion. Privacy as a feature is fine. Privacy as a moat is
a story you're telling yourself to feel good.

Apple is making privacy table stakes. ATT, on-device intelligence,
the whole Apple Intelligence push — they're commoditizing your
differentiator. In two years "on-device AI" will be the default in
every productivity app and you'll have nothing to lean on.

### Moat 3: "Character system creates sunk cost"

Habitica, Finch, Forest's tree forest — all already do gamified
progression with character/avatar mechanics. You're not first. You
might be more polished, sure. Polish is not a moat.

### Moat 4: "Open-source coach engine"

Cute. Why does this matter to a user buying a focus timer? It doesn't.
It matters to nerds reading your README on GitHub. That's not a
buyer.

The one thing this MIGHT do is recruit other indie devs who want to
extend the engine for their own apps. That's a **B2B angle** you
haven't even acknowledged. License the coach engine to wellness
companies, productivity SaaS, enterprise mental health platforms.
That's a real business. The B2C app is your demo.

**Real defensible thing:** the brand voice. "Built honestly. No
slop." If you become recognized as the indie productivity brand
that takes craft seriously, that's a moat — but only if you build
distribution at the same time.

**Action:** stop calling features "moats." A feature ships in 6
weeks. A moat takes 5 years. The actual moat is reputation +
distribution. Build those.

## 5 · Why now? Be specific.

"AI backlash" is a real cultural moment. But it's diffuse. There's
no clear "AI fatigue" buyer waiting for your app.

The real "why now" is:
- Apple Intelligence in 2024-2025 made on-device AI a category
- Indie iOS dev is having a renaissance (App Store policies, smaller
  teams, faster build tools)
- Pomodoro nostalgia + the dopamine-seeking trends in productivity
  content

But none of these are sharp enough to define a launch window. You
could launch this in 2024, 2026, or 2028 and the timing argument
sounds the same.

**Action:** can you point to ONE thing that's true right now that
wasn't true 18 months ago, that makes FocusForge winnable now in a
way it wouldn't have been before? If not, your timing isn't an
advantage.

## 6 · Your retention targets are fiction

D1 ≥45%, D7 ≥20%, D30 ≥18%. These are great numbers. They're also
**dreams**. You have zero launch data.

Top quartile iOS productivity apps hit D7 ~18-22%. The MEDIAN is
D7 ~10%. You're targeting top quartile from cold start.

I'd bet you hit D7 of 12-15% in your first cohort. Maybe 18% if you
nail the onboarding. Then you'll spend three months chasing the
last 2-3 points.

**That's fine.** But don't pretend the targets are calibrated. They're
aspirational. Treat them as direction, not goal.

The real question: what's your **pivot trigger**? You have one in
the PRD ("D30 < 5% = re-evaluate thesis"). Good. But what's your
**iterate** trigger between 10-18%? What specific change do you make?
Have you sketched A/B tests? Backup plans?

**Action:** for every retention bucket (D30 5%, 10-15%, 15-18%,
18-22%, 22%+), write down what you do next. Three concrete moves
per bucket. So when the data comes in you're not flat-footed.

## 7 · The hidden positive — your engine is more valuable than your app

Here's the thing nobody seems to be saying: **the coach engine is
worth more than the app.**

Think about it:
- Enterprise wellness companies (Calm Business, Headspace for Work,
  ginger.io) all need on-device, privacy-compliant AI coaching
  modules. None of them have built one. You have.
- HIPAA-compliant productivity for healthcare workers requires
  on-device only. You're already there.
- Insurance companies offering mental-health benefits want
  branded-but-private wellness experiences. You have the infrastructure.

The B2C app is a demo. The engine is the asset.

I'm not saying drop the B2C app. I'm saying **don't bet everything on
B2C.** Build out the engine's documentation, get one B2B pilot
customer (even free), and use that as the credibility ladder for
both businesses. The B2C app validates the engine. The engine
monetizes.

**Action:** sketch a "FocusForge Coach Engine — B2B Pilot Proposal"
doc. Reach out to 3-5 enterprise wellness companies. See if anyone
bites. You can do this in parallel with the App Store launch.

## 8 · Where's your CAC / LTV?

You don't have either yet. That's fine for v1.0. But you should be
able to estimate.

**Estimated CAC (organic only, year 1):** $2-5 per install if your
X strategy works (twitter following + product hunt + indie press
mentions). Could be lower if a tweet goes viral. Could be $0 for
your first 500 users (the existing followers).

**Estimated LTV:** at current $0 ARPU, **LTV = $0**.

Even if you ship FocusForge+ at $5/mo with 5% conversion and 12-month
average lifespan:
- LTV = $5 × 12 × 0.05 = $3 per install
- That's *below* your estimated CAC

**That's a non-business.** You'd be acquiring users at a loss.

To make this work you need at least one of:
- Higher conversion (10%+ to paid)
- Higher ARPU ($10/mo or $99/yr)
- Lower CAC (organic viral coefficient)
- Longer LTV (multi-year retention)

**Action:** model these. Build a simple spreadsheet:
- Column 1: free → trial conversion at 20%, 30%, 40%
- Column 2: trial → paid conversion at 10%, 20%, 30%
- Column 3: average paid lifespan at 6/12/24 months
- Column 4: resulting LTV
- Column 5: CAC scenarios

If no combination gives LTV > 3× CAC, you don't have a unit-economic
business. Period. Doesn't matter how good the app feels.

## 9 · The honest path forward

Three paths. Pick one.

### Path A: lifestyle business (most honest)

Ship as-is. Add FocusForge+ at $4.99/mo. Get to 1,000 paid subscribers
in year one. $50K/yr revenue. You keep your day job or freelance to
supplement. The app becomes a steady-state passion project that pays
for itself plus a little.

This is fine. **Most apps are this. There's nothing wrong with it.**
Just be honest with yourself.

### Path B: scale attempt (longest shot)

Raise a small angel round ($250K-500K). Use it for:
- Paid acquisition ($150K)
- Press / creator partnerships ($50K)
- Part-time engineer to ship faster ($100K)
- 12-month runway

Target: 100K MAU by month 18. 10% paid = 10K paid. $500K ARR. That
gets you a Series A conversation.

Hard. Probably won't work. But it's the actual venture path.

### Path C: B2B engine (most interesting)

Position FocusForge as the demo for the **Coach Engine** as a B2B
product. Cold-outreach to wellness companies. Land 2-3 pilot
customers. License the engine at $10K-50K per customer per year.

The B2C app keeps running but as marketing for the engine. You're
not competing with Forest anymore — you're a supplier to wellness
platforms that don't want to build their own AI coach.

This is what I'd actually invest in if you came to me. The B2B
angle is novel, the engine is real, the privacy story sells in
healthcare/enterprise.

## 10 · My actual advice if you had 60 seconds

1. **Ship the subscription on day one.** No exceptions.
2. **Stop calling features moats.** Brand and distribution are moats.
   Build those.
3. **Pick your business model in 24 hours.** Path A, B, or C. Halfway
   between any two is the death zone.
4. **Set up a CRM and start emailing wellness companies tomorrow.**
   Even if you don't pursue B2B fully, having 5 conversations will
   sharpen your B2C pitch.
5. **Don't ship without paid distribution planning.** Organic-only is
   not a strategy. It's a wish.
6. **Reframe your retention targets as hypotheses, not goals.** Plan
   what you do when they're wrong.
7. **Open-source the engine more aggressively.** Get it in Hacker
   News, dev.to, indie hacker newsletters. Recruit other devs to
   extend it. THAT is your distribution flywheel — not your 1,000
   X followers.

## Where I disagree with the staff review

The senior staff review was tactical-good. Submit, ship, iterate.
Fix the iPad family, fix the analytics events, fix the age rating.
All correct.

What it doesn't address is **whether any of this matters if the
business model isn't there.**

You can polish the icon, perfect the screenshots, write beautiful
privacy policies, and have a hand-tuned 5-beat reward animation —
and still have zero revenue and zero users in six months. The
staff review treats v1.0 like an engineering deliverable. It is
that. But it's also a **commercial bet** that should be evaluated
on commercial terms.

The polish is real. The craft is real. Both matter to a subset of
users. But neither solves: **how do you make money? How do you find
users? Why does this still exist in 12 months?**

## What I'd actually fund

If you walked into Shark Tank tomorrow with this exact deliverable —
no monetization, no distribution plan, solo dev, $0 raised, hosted on
your personal portfolio site, free in App Store — I'd pass.

Not because it's bad. Because **it's not a business yet.**

What would change my mind:
- Subscription live at launch with a defensible price point
- One signed LOI from a B2B pilot customer (even unpaid)
- A 6-month paid acquisition plan with a real budget
- 10K X followers, not 1K (showing distribution muscle, not just
  build muscle)

You can have all of these in 90 days if you commit.
